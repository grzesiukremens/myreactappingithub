## 1. EU AI Act Article 50: does it apply from 2 August 2026, what changed with the Digital Omnibus, what must deployers do, which guidance exists, and who enforces it in Poland?

### Takeaway
Article 50 transparency obligations were **not** postponed. They have applied since **2 August 2026**. The Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force since 27 July 2026) only gave a grace period until **2 December 2026** to *providers* of generative systems already on the market, and only for the machine-readable watermarking duty in Art. 50(2). As an advertiser publishing realistic AI-generated or AI-manipulated video, image or audio, Vellano is a "deployer" under **Art. 50(4)**. It must visibly disclose that the content is AI-generated or manipulated, at the latest at first exposure. The Commission's final guidelines (20 July 2026) read "deepfake" broadly: a **photorealistic invented person** counts, and ads generally do **not** benefit from the lighter "artistic/creative" regime. In Poland, the supervisory authority is **KRiBSI** (Komisja Rozwoju i Bezpieczeństwa Sztucznej Inteligencji), set up by the Act of 3 July 2026 (Dz.U. 2026 poz. 1003). Its inspection and penalty powers start **28 October 2026**.

### Cited Findings

**Digital Omnibus: what was and wasn't postponed**
- A provisional political agreement on the AI Omnibus between Council and Parliament was reached on 7 May 2026 — [Dastra](https://www.dastra.eu/en/blog/simpler-safer-stricter-where-it-counts-inside-the-eu-ai-omnibus-deal/60025); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/) (date from search summary).
- The amending act is **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744**. Per commentary, it was adopted on 8 July 2026, published in the OJ on 24 July 2026, and entered into force on 27 July 2026 — [EUR-Lex 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng); [Cypheron](https://cypheron.cz/en/resources/ai-act/omnibus-2026); [Haqq](https://www.haqq.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-amendments-2026); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/). Note: one June source still described formal adoption as pending. The later sources agree on entry into force on 27 July 2026.
- High-risk deadlines moved: Annex III stand-alone high-risk systems from 2 Aug 2026 to **2 Dec 2027**, and Annex I to **2 Aug 2028** — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/the-ai-act-implementation-timeline-what-changes-under-the-ai-omnibus/); [Technology.org, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.technology.org/2026/10/13/eu-ai-act-deadlines-after-the-digital-omnibus-what-applies-from-december-2026-to-august-2028/).
- **Article 50 was not postponed** and applies from 2 August 2026 — [AI Act Blog NL: "the AI Act deadline of 2 August 2026 that has not been postponed"](https://www.aiactblog.nl/en/posts/article-50-transparency-deadline-2-august-2026); [AIDIA](https://aidia.it/en/news/digital-omnibus-ai-act-cosa-slitta-2-agosto-2026/); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/).
- The only Art. 50 carve-out is the new Art. 111(4). Providers of systems generating synthetic audio, image, video or text that were placed on the market **before 2 Aug 2026** must comply with **Art. 50(2)** (machine-readable marking) by **2 Dec 2026**. The EUR-Lex recitals describe a "four-month transitional period". The deferral does not cover the rest of Art. 50 — [EUR-Lex 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng); [Cypheron](https://cypheron.cz/en/resources/ai-act/omnibus-2026); [Technology.org](https://www.technology.org/2026/10/13/eu-ai-act-deadlines-after-the-digital-omnibus-what-applies-from-december-2026-to-august-2028/).
- Caveat flagged by the search summary: FPF reportedly mislabels Art. 50(2) as covering GPAI models. Art. 50(2) actually targets providers of generative AI systems — [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/the-ai-act-implementation-timeline-what-changes-under-the-ai-omnibus/).

**What the deployer must do (Art. 50(4) plus the definition of "deep fake")**
- Statutory definition, Art. 3(60): "'deep fake' means AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video content that resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities or events and would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful" — quoted via [article50.io](https://article50.io/article-50-deepfake-disclosure); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nbz0/transparency-obligations-for-ai-generated-content-the-code-of-practice-adequacy/).
- Professional users of AI, including advertising and marketing companies, must inform viewers that an image, video or sound was generated or manipulated by AI. The information must be visible **at the latest at first contact** with the content. The only exemption is purely personal, non-professional activity — [Prawo.pl](https://www.prawo.pl/biznes/ai-act-kto-musi-oznaczac-tresci-od-2-sierpnia,1548339.html).
- The obligation sits with the deployer (the user of the AI system), not the tool maker. One Polish commentary gives the example of a graphic designer generating an advertising deepfake for a business, and places the Art. 50(4) duty on that designer — [GazetaPrawna](https://www.gazetaprawna.pl/nowe-technologie/ai/artykuly/11283612,ai-act-2-sierpnia-2026-oznaczanie-deepfake-chatboty.html) (from search summary). See Inferences on how this splits between agency and advertiser.
- **Commission guidelines on Art. 50:** draft released 8 May 2026 for consultation — [Global Policy Watch (Covington)](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/05/10-takeaways-european-commission-draft-guidelines-on-ai-transparency-under-the-eu-ai-act/). **Final guidelines adopted 20 July 2026**, non-binding (only the CJEU interprets authoritatively) — [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/european-commission-adopts-final-guidelines-on-ai-act-article-50-transparency-obligations-first-impr); [Stibbe](https://www.stibbe.com/publications-and-insights/the-ai-acts-transparency-obligations-rules-scope-and-timeline); [McCann FitzGerald (Part 2: deployer obligations)](https://www.mccannfitzgerald.com/knowledge/data-privacy-and-cyber-risk/ai-transparency-european-commissions-guidelines-on-article-50-part-2-deployer-obligations). Conflict: the [artificialintelligenceact.eu guide](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/) still described the guidelines as a draft. Rely on the 20 July adoption reported by multiple law firms.
- **Invented people count.** The final guidelines read "existing" broadly. Content resembling something that *could plausibly exist*, "such as a photorealistic portrait of an invented person", stays within the deepfake definition. The Commission declined industry requests to narrow this — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nbz0/transparency-obligations-for-ai-generated-content-the-code-of-practice-adequacy/); [article50.io](https://article50.io/article-50-deepfake-disclosure); [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/european-commission-adopts-final-guidelines-on-ai-act-article-50-transparency-obligations-first-impr) (search summary). Conflict: a Polish piece reasoned from the bare definition that fully synthetic faces "may not" be covered — [ai.infor.pl](https://ai.infor.pl/sztuczna-inteligencja/7647850,oznaczanie-tresci-ai-co-trzeba-wiedziec-o-nowym-obowiazku-wywiad.html). That inference predates or ignores the final guidelines.
- **Intent does not matter.** Whether content falsely appears authentic is judged regardless of the deployer's intention to deceive, so lack of fraudulent intent does not remove the labelling duty — [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/european-commission-adopts-final-guidelines-on-ai-act-article-50-transparency-obligations-first-impr); [McCann FitzGerald](https://www.mccannfitzgerald.com/knowledge/data-privacy-and-cyber-risk/ai-transparency-european-commissions-guidelines-on-article-50-part-2-deployer-obligations) (search summary).
- **Machine-readable marks are not enough.** Disclosure must be perceivable. Deployers cannot rely on the provider's Art. 50(2) watermark/C2PA — [Commission FAQ on Art. 50](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act); [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/european-commission-adopts-final-guidelines-on-ai-act-article-50-transparency-obligations-first-impr).
- **Timing and placement:** first exposure is the trigger, e.g. the opening of a deepfake video. A single up-front notice may not suffice for video or audio where viewers can miss the start. Labels should be immediately recognisable, visible long enough, and embedded in the content where possible — [Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content). Some guides say video labels should persist — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/mdodeja/labelling-ai-written-articles-and-synthetic-media-under-article-50-3b70) (low authority). Visual labels suit image/video and audible disclosure suits audio-only content — [Legalithm](https://www.legalithm.com/en/blog/ai-act-transparency-obligations-article-50-deepfake-labeling).
- **The creative/artistic regime is narrow and generally excludes ads.** For "evidently" artistic, creative, satirical or fictional works, disclosure is still required but may be made in a way that does not hamper enjoyment of the work — [Commission FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act); [Stibbe](https://www.stibbe.com/publications-and-insights/deep-fakes-real-transparency-labelling-deep-fakes-and-ai-generated-public). The guidelines' own non-qualifying examples are advertisements: a **teleshopping-style video with simulated people demonstrating a product** and a **synthetic influencer testing a sponsored product**. Where content is both informative/commercial and creative, the informative character prevails — [Numonic](https://www.numonic.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-article-50-content-creators); [Argnode](https://www.argnode.com/en/articles/eu-ai-content-labelling); [DILR](https://www.dilr.ai/blog/studio-eu-ai-act-article-50-synthetic-content); [IP Global Guard](https://ipglobalguard.com/en/blog/ai-act-article-50-guidelines-deepfakes/). A surreal brand film with an obviously fictional premise might qualify — [Numonic](https://www.numonic.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-article-50-content-creators).
- Davis+Gilbert advises clearly labelling realistic AI-generated creative used in market, "including human and non-human characters". Its examples include an AI depiction of a celebrity influencer and a realistic synthetic influencer testing a sponsored product — [Davis+Gilbert](https://www.dglaw.com/eu-ai-act-guidance-expands-ai-disclosure-rules-for-advertisers-and-pr-teams/).
- **Stylised content and products:** mascots, talking animals and clearly unrealistic imagery (dragons, people flying unaided) generally fall outside the deepfake definition — [DILR](https://www.dilr.ai/blog/studio-eu-ai-act-article-50-synthetic-content); [Peterka Partners](https://blog.peterkapartners.com/article-50-of-the-ai-act-in-practice-transparency-of-ai-systems-content-labeling-and-deepfakes/). An AI product image that makes the product look better than in reality, or that "may influence the perception of its actual appearance, characteristics, or use", is likely within scope. Colour correction and aesthetic background changes are not — [Peterka Partners](https://blog.peterkapartners.com/article-50-of-the-ai-act-in-practice-transparency-of-ai-systems-content-labeling-and-deepfakes/); [IP Global Guard](https://ipglobalguard.com/en/blog/ai-act-article-50-guidelines-deepfakes/) (search summary; exact attribution between these two uncertain).
- **Voice:** guidance lists AI-manipulated audio with voice cloning of real presenters as a labelling-required example — [Peterka Partners](https://blog.peterkapartners.com/article-50-of-the-ai-act-in-practice-transparency-of-ai-systems-content-labeling-and-deepfakes/); [Travers Smith](https://www.traverssmith.com/knowledge/knowledge-container/is-it-a-bot-eu-ai-act-transparency-rules-take-effect-2-august-2026/) (search summary). A generic synthetic narrator not imitating a real person was not addressed in the sources found.
- **Territorial reach:** the guidelines suggest that posting deepfakes "on the globally accessible internet" can bring content in scope — [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/european-commission-adopts-final-guidelines-on-ai-act-article-50-transparency-obligations-first-impr) (search summary).
- Content published **before** 2 Aug 2026 does not need retroactive labelling — [Deepfake Policy](https://deepfakepolicy.com/guides/eu-ai-act-deepfake-labelling-article-50) (secondary).

**Code of Practice on marking and labelling AI-generated content**
- The Commission published the **final Code of Practice on 10 June 2026** — [European Commission press release](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-publishes-code-practice-marking-and-labelling-ai-generated-content); [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/06/european-commission-publishes-final-code-of-practice-on-marking-and-labelling-aigenerated-content); [CEPIS](https://cepis.org/european-commission-publishes-final-ai-content-labelling-code-of-practice/). The first draft had come earlier — [Commission (first draft)](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-publishes-first-draft-code-practice-marking-and-labelling-ai-generated-content); [Kirkland, Feb 2026](https://www.kirkland.com/publications/kirkland-alert/2026/02/illuminating-ai-the-eus-first-draft-code-of-practice-on-transparency-for-ai).
- The Code is **voluntary**. It adds no obligations and excuses none: Art. 50 applies on the same terms whether a company signs or not. It is open for signature and being assessed for adequacy by the Commission and AI Board — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/06/european-commission-publishes-final-code-of-practice-on-marking-and-labelling-aigenerated-content); [NicFab](https://www.nicfab.eu/en/posts/code-of-practice-transparency-ai-content/). A Reed Smith post title refers to a "Code of Practice adequacy decision", but details were not verified — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nbz0/transparency-obligations-for-ai-generated-content-the-code-of-practice-adequacy/).
- Structure: Section 1 covers marking and detection (providers). Section 2 covers labelling of deepfakes and public-interest text (deployers), including how and where to show labels and the publicly available **EU icon** or equivalent labels — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/06/european-commission-publishes-final-code-of-practice-on-marking-and-labelling-aigenerated-content).
- The Code provides a basic AI icon plus "fully AI-generated" and "partially AI-modified" variants, and encourages a second-layer word ("generated"/"modified") next to the icon. For **non-real-time deepfake video**, signatories use the icon **combined with a disclaimer at the beginning of the exposure**. For real-time video, the icon appears non-intrusively throughout where feasible. For evidently artistic/fictional works, disclosure may sit in end credits — [Heuking](https://www.heuking.de/en/news-events/newsletter-articles/detail/the-european-commission-specifies-labelling-requirements-for-ai-generated-content-and-deepfakes.html); [TechPolicy.Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/what-the-eus-new-ai-code-of-practice-means-for-labeling-deepfakes/); [DLA Piper, July 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/what-the-eus-new-code-of-practice-means). Using the icon does not by itself guarantee compliance — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/what-the-eus-new-code-of-practice-means).

### Inferences
- Nearly every planned Vellano format with realistic AI-generated humans counts as a deepfake under the guidelines' broad reading: the hoodie "3-2-1" reveal, a person putting the chain on, talking UGC-style AI actors, and gift-reaction skits. Each needs a **visible, in-video label at first exposure**, ideally persisting or repeated, plus an audible mention where the voice carries the message. A safe Polish wording is e.g. "Wideo wygenerowane przez AI" or "Postacie i głos wygenerowane przez AI", plus the EU icon.
- The friends' photos animated by AI are the clearest deepfake case: a real, existing person is shown doing something they did not do. Label them, and see Q5 on consent.
- AI voiceovers in Polish: a realistic synthetic human voice likely falls within "audio resembling existing persons" under the plausibility reading. Treat it as requiring disclosure. Low-cost fix: add "głos lektora wygenerowany przez AI" in the label.
- Product animations of the pendant: a clearly stylised 3D or "magic" animation is probably not a deepfake. A **photorealistic** AI render that could pass for real footage of the product is in scope, especially if it shows the rhinestones, shine or size better than the real item. That also creates a separate misleading-advertising risk (Q4). Safest: base product shots on real photography and keep AI to backgrounds and motion. Otherwise label.
- Agency vs advertiser: if an agency or freelancer generates the clips, both may be treated as deployers (Polish commentary puts the duty on the designer). Vellano publishes the ad under its own name and controls the use, so it should contractually require labelling and also label itself.
- Old (pre-August 2026) organic posts need no retroactive label. Re-using them as **new ads** after 2 Aug 2026 is a new exposure and should be labelled.
- An SME's realistic maximum exposure under Art. 99 is "3% of turnover or €15m, whichever is lower". The practical enforcement risk in late 2026 is more likely platform rejection plus UOKiK unfair-practice exposure than a KRiBSI fine, but this is speculative.

## 2. TikTok rules in 2026: AIGC labelling for organic posts and ads, AI avatars, digital likeness, C2PA, and depicting non-existent people as customers

### Takeaway
TikTok allows AI-generated or significantly AI-edited ads **only if disclosed**. In Ads Manager, the advertiser switches on the mandatory **AI-generated content disclaimer** (shown at the bottom of in-feed ads) or adds its own clear caption, watermark or sticker. Undisclosed AIGC is rejected or restricted. Spark Ads (boosted organic posts) instead rely on the organic **AIGC label** set on the post. A label does not make harmful content acceptable. TikTok bars AI content that falsely shows public figures endorsing products, or that uses the likeness of minors or private adults without permission. TikTok auto-labels content carrying C2PA Content Credentials. A business's promotional organic posts also need the **Commercial Content Disclosure** toggle, which is separate from the AI label.

### Cited Findings
- Official ad-disclaimer page: "Mandatory disclaimers: AI-generated, synthetic, or manipulated media. This includes images, video, or audio that are completely AI-generated or real source material that has been significantly modified by AI." The AI-generated content disclaimer adds a textual label stating the ad includes AI content — [TikTok Ads Help: About ad disclaimers](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-ad-disclaimers-in-tiktok-ads-manager).
- Significantly edited media and AIGC are allowed if the advertiser applies the AIGC label or adds "a clear disclaimer, caption, watermark, or sticker of your own". Minor edits (lighting, background removal) do not count. "If we identify AI-generated content that has not been disclosed, your ad will be rejected or restricted." — [TikTok Ads Policy: Misleading and false content](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-misleading-and-false-content); [About ad disclaimers](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-ad-disclaimers-in-tiktok-ads-manager).
- Toggle mechanics: it appears at the bottom of in-feed ads. After submission it cannot be unselected unless the video or image is changed. Any new campaign creation, **including duplication, resets the toggle**. Spark Ads are not covered by the Ads Manager toggle and follow the Community Guidelines' AI disclosure rules — [TikTok Ads Help: Add disclaimers to ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/add-disclaimers-to-ads); [About ad disclaimers](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-ad-disclaimers-in-tiktok-ads-manager).
- TikTok's advertiser blog confirms a self-disclosure toggle in Ads Manager to declare an ad AI-generated — [TikTok Business blog: Enhancing privacy and control](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/enhancing-privacy-control-advertisers-users); see also [Ensuring creative integrity in the age of generative AI](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-integrity-generative-ai).
- **Labels are not a permission slip.** "TikTok's policies restrict harmful uses of AIGC even when content is appropriately labeled." Repeat offenders may be suspended — [Misleading and false content](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-misleading-and-false-content); [Deceptive Practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-deceptive-practices).
- No general TikTok ad rule specifically on fabricated testimonials was found. Testimonial rules found were market-specific (healthcare/aesthetics). Once content is auto-labelled, the label cannot be removed — [TikTok Support: AI-generated content](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content); [TikTok Creator Academy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/ai-generated-content-label). ### Inferences
- AI avatars and AI "actors" are **allowed** in TikTok ads if (a) the AIGC disclaimer is switched on, ideally with an in-video label too, and (b) they do not imitate any real person without permission.
- Depicting a non-existent person as a **real customer** is not explicitly listed as banned, but it fits squarely within "misleading/deceptive" claims. It is also the core consumer-law problem (Q4). Do not script AI actors as genuine buyers.
- Operational checklist: switch the AIGC toggle on for every ad, and **re-check after duplicating campaigns** because duplication resets it. For Spark Ads, set the AIGC label on the organic post before boosting. Add "Your brand" commercial-content disclosure on organic promo posts. Keep C2PA metadata intact where the tool embeds it, since auto-labels can't be removed but do help compliance.
- Animated friends: allowed only with their documented permission. Without it, this is prohibited "likeness of private adults" content even when labelled.

### Gaps

- No official TikTok statement found on whether the in-feed AI disclaimer alone satisfies AI Act Art. 50(4). It is a platform label, possibly not at "first exposure" or not in Polish, so an in-video label is the safe add-on.

## 3. Meta rules in 2026: "AI info" labels, advertiser disclosure for digitally created or altered ads, AI testimonials and people

### Takeaway
Meta's **mandatory advertiser AI disclosure** officially applies only to **social-issue, electoral and political ads** (since January 2024). It covers realistic, digitally created or altered people or events, including "a realistic-looking person that does not exist". For ordinary commercial ads, Meta **automatically** applies "AI info" when its own generative ad tools were used, and may detect C2PA metadata. Ads made with third-party AI tools are not necessarily labelled by Meta. A Polish jewelry ad made with external AI tools will therefore often carry **no Meta label**, so the AI Act Art. 50 label must be built into the creative. General Meta ad standards (deception, IP) still apply.

### Cited Findings
- Political/social-issue ads: advertisers must disclose when they use AI to create or alter such ads that contain realistic content digitally created or altered to depict a real person saying or doing something they did not, **a realistic-looking person that does not exist**, or a realistic event that did not happen. Meta then adds information to the ad and Ad Library. In effect since January 2024 — [Meta Newsroom, Nov 2023](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/11/how-meta-is-planning-for-elections-in-2024/); [Meta Newsroom, Feb 2026 (US midterms)](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/02/meta-prepares-for-2026-us-midterms/).
- Commercial ads: ads "fully created or materially edited using Meta's generative AI creative features available via our marketing tools will have AI info applied on or within the ad". The label usually sits in the three-dot menu, but for an AI-generated photorealistic human it appears next to "Sponsored". Minor edits (resizing, colour correction) do not count. Ads made with third-party creation or AI tools are not labelled this way. Rollout is gradual — [Meta Help Center: How AI-generated images in ads are identified and labelled](https://www.meta.com/en-gb/help/artificial-intelligence/355108217670024/).
- Organic content: users must use Meta's AI-disclosure tool for organic posts with photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered, "and we may apply penalties if they fail to do so" — [Meta Community Standards: Misinformation](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/misinformation/).
- Meta reads industry metadata (C2PA/IPTC) to label content from other AI generators — [CineRads](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/ai-ugc-facebook-ad-policy) (secondary). Reported dates for automatic ad labelling vary between June and July 2026 — [Coinis](https://coinis.com/blog/meta-ai-content-labeling-facebook-instagram-ads-2026); [AdPlay](https://adplay.ai/blog/made-with-ai-label-meta-ads) (secondary, conflicting).
- Several SEO roundups claim Meta now mandates AI disclosure on **all** ads. A fact-check by Digital Applied says Meta's primary guidance does not support that; only social-issue/electoral/political ads require self-disclosure — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-content-labeling-rules-advertisers-2026-reference). Other blogs describe an Ads Manager toggle for marking an ad as AI-generated — [Coinis](https://coinis.com/blog/meta-ai-content-labeling-facebook-instagram-ads-2026); [AdriseLab](https://adriselab.com/blog/meta-ads-ai-disclosure-rules-2026) (unverified, conflicting).
- No Meta announcement of an **EU-specific** AI Act labelling feature for ads (August 2026) was found — search across [e-dialog](https://e-dialog.group/en/blog/the-eu-ai-act-is-coming-heres-how-to-stay-on-the-safe-side-with-your-ad-campaigns/), [CineRads](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/ai-ad-disclosure-requirements) and others.
- Reportedly, the running advertiser is responsible for AI-generated variants even when Meta's tools created them — [82dash](https://blog.82dash.com/ai-ad-label-rules-meta-google-tiktok/) (secondary).
- AI testimonials: no specific published Meta rule on AI-generated testimonials was found. Commentators say Meta's general standards against deceptive practices and fake testimonials apply. An AI persona presented as a real customer is deceptive, while a synthetic presenter clearly acting as a brand spokesperson is generally acceptable — [CineRads](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/ai-ugc-facebook-ad-policy); [UGCVids](https://ugcvids.ai/blog/meta-ai-generated-creative-ad-policy-2026); [Starpop](https://starpop.ai/blog/articles/are-ai-generated-ads-allowed-on-meta) (all secondary).

### Inferences
- For Meta, Vellano is not obliged by Meta policy to self-declare AI in a commercial jewelry ad, but **EU law requires it** (Q1). Burn the label into the video (first frame plus persistent corner tag) and, optionally, add it to the primary text. Use Meta's AI toggle where available.
- Do not run ads through the "social issues/politics" category; it is irrelevant here.
- Keep a "brand spokesperson/skit" framing for AI actors (Q4). Meta reviewers and UOKiK would both see "fake customer" framing as deceptive.

### Gaps
- Meta's live Advertising Standards pages (e.g., "Unacceptable business practices", IP, "Fraud, scams and deceptive practices") could not be read. Exact 2026 wording on synthetic testimonials is unverified.
- Unclear whether Meta introduced an advertiser AI toggle for all ads in the EU after 2 Aug 2026 (sources conflict).

## 4. Polish/EU consumer law on fake testimonials and reviews: UOKiK influencer guidance, Omnibus fake-review bans, UPNPR; is an AI actor saying "I bought this and love it" misleading; how to frame AI "UGC"; UOKiK on AI in advertising

### Takeaway
Under the **ustawa o przeciwdziałaniu nieuczciwym praktykom rynkowym** (UPNPR, implementing the UCPD as amended by the Omnibus Directive 2019/2161), posting or commissioning **false consumer reviews or recommendations** is a practice banned in all circumstances (black list points 23b/23c). Presenting commercial content as independent consumer opinion is also misleading. An AI actor saying "I bought this and love it" is a fabricated first-person consumer experience. It very likely counts as a misleading practice, or a false recommendation, **even with an "AI" label**. UOKiK has no AI-specific decision on synthetic testimonials (none found). Its line is that AI use is not unlawful per se; the issue is misleading consumers. UOKiK's 2022 influencer recommendations require clear, Polish-language, two-level labelling of advertising. The safe frame is an openly branded **skit or presentation**: "reklama Vellano", "scenka", "postacie wygenerowane przez AI", with the brand speaking, not a fake customer.

### Cited Findings
- Omnibus added black-list points to UCPD Annex I. **23b**: stating that reviews come from real consumers without taking reasonable and proportionate steps to verify it. **23c**: "zamieszczanie lub zlecenie innej osobie prawnej lub fizycznej zamieszczania nieprawdziwych opinii konsumentów lub rekomendacji lub zniekształcanie opinii konsumentów" in order to promote products — [PARP: Dyrektywa Omnibus – opinie](https://www.parp.gov.pl/component/content/article/83340:dyrektywa-omnibus-nowe-obowiazki-dla-e-sprzedawcow-oraz-internetowych-platform-handlowych); [dlahandlu.pl](https://www.dlahandlu.pl/e-commerce/falszywe-opinie-na-stronach-e-sklepow-a-dyrektywa-omnibus,110342.html); [Prokonsumencki.pl](https://prokonsumencki.pl/blog/dyrektywa-omnibus-wszystko-o-opiniach-i-rekomendacjach-zgodnie-z-nowymi-przepisami/). Consolidated UPNPR text: Dz.U. 2023 poz. 845 — [ISAP](https://isap.sejm.gov.pl/isap.nsf/DocDetails.xsp?id=WDU20230000845); [ELI text](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2023/845/text.html). (The 23b/23c wording quoted by Prokonsumencki came from a June 2022 draft; verify against the consolidated text.)
- UCPD Annex I point 22 bans "falsely claiming or creating the impression that the trader is not acting for purposes relating to his trade… or falsely representing oneself as a consumer" — [digitalfairnessact.com: UCPD](https://digitalfairnessact.com/unfair-commercial-practices-directive-ucpd); [Hadi Vahidi](https://hadivahidi.com/blog/are-ai-ugc-ads-legal-in-europe). Commentary notes this is a loose fit for a synthetic avatar. The general misleading-practice prohibition is the stronger basis — [Hadi Vahidi](https://hadivahidi.com/blog/are-ai-ugc-ads-legal-in-europe).
- Commentators: an "AI-generated" label does not make a fabricated testimonial, false product claim or unauthorised endorsement lawful. In synthetic UGC "no genuine product experience took place", viewers may think it authentic, and claimed results may be unsupported — [Make Influence](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/ai-generated-synthetic-ugc-ads-when-a-brand-uses-an-ai-actor-instead-of-a-real-creator); [Oakgen](https://oakgen.ai/blog/ai-ugc-legal-disclosure-checklist-2026) (secondary). US comparison: the FTC rule bans fake consumer testimonials misrepresenting the reviewer's identity, experience or existence, and legal analysis says AI avatars delivering testimonials likely fall within it — [Daily Intel Service](https://dailyintelservice.com/future/are-ai-ugc-testimonial-ads-legal-ftc-rules-for-2026); [AdsGen](https://adsgen.ai/blog/are-ai-ugc-ads-legal). No EU equivalent rule specific to AI was found.
- **UOKiK fake-review enforcement:** Opinie.pro and SN Marketing were the first companies fined by the UOKiK President for trading in fake reviews and ratings. UOKiK runs a "Fake opinions? Stop!" campaign — [UOKiK: Kary za fałszywe opinie](https://archiwum.uokik.gov.pl/aktualnosci.php?news_id=19139); [UOKiK EN: Fake opinions? Stop!](https://uokik.gov.pl/en/fake-opinions-stop); [UOKiK: Tailor-made reviews](https://uokik.gov.pl/en/tailor-made-reviews-uokik-president-presses-charges-against-best-review). UOKiK tells consumers that reviews from accounts with photos of models or fictitious accounts should raise suspicion — [UOKiK: Fake opinions? Stop!](https://uokik.gov.pl/en/fake-opinions-stop).
- **UOKiK influencer recommendations (Sept 2022):** **two-level labelling** (in the content itself, e.g. description, image, video or narration, *and* via the platform's tool). Labels must be visible to regular and first-time viewers, in **Polish** if the profile is in Polish, and must name the brand. The rule is "nie jesteś pewien – oznacz". Responsibility for correct labelling lies with influencers, **agencies and advertisers** — [Rekomendacje Prezesa UOKiK (PDF)](https://uokik.gov.pl/download/26685); [IAB copy](https://www.iab.org.pl/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Rekomendacje_Prezesa_UOKiK.pdf); [mikroPorady](https://mikroporady.pl/porady/rekomendacje-prezesa-uokik-dotyczace-oznaczania-tresci-reklamowych-przez-influencerow); [WhitePress](https://www.whitepress.com/pl/baza-wiedzy/760/oznaczanie-wspolprac-przez-influencerow-nowe-wytyczne-uokik).
- UOKiK's report on unfair influencer marketing mentions **virtual influencers (avatars)** as a growing phenomenon but sets no separate rules for them — [UOKiK: Ochrona konsumentów przed nieuczciwym marketingiem influencerów](https://uokik.gov.pl/Download/1617).
- UOKiK enforcement against influencers includes a fine of over PLN 5 million for surreptitious advertising on Instagram — [UOKiK EN](https://uokik.gov.pl/en/over-5-million-fine-for-surreptitious-advertising-on-instagram) — and actions against influencers promoting "scam" products — [UOKiK archive](https://archiwum.uokik.gov.pl/aktualnosci.php?news_id=18048).
- **UOKiK on AI:** no UOKiK statement or decision on deepfake or AI-avatar advertising was found for 2025–2026. A secondary source quotes UOKiK as saying "using artificial intelligence alone does not constitute a violation of consumer rights"; the problem arises when AI misleads — [Poradnik Przedsiębiorcy](https://poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl/-sztuczna-inteligencja-w-sklepie-kto-odpowiada-za-bledy-w-opisach) (quote unverified against UOKiK primary text). UOKiK uses its own AI tool (DRAKE) to detect dark patterns such as fake countdown timers — [Kamil Siedlikowski](https://kamilsiedlikowski.pl/aktualnosci/uokik-drake-dark-patterns-ai.html); [Executive Magazine](https://executivemagazine.pl/nowe-technologie/uokik-i-sztuczna-inteligencja-walka-z-dark-patterns/). Unlabelled AI visuals that mislead consumers (e.g., AI virtual staging) are flagged as an unfair-practice risk — [CGwisdom](https://cgwisdom.pl/blog/czy-musisz-oznaczac-wizualizacje-jako-ai-co-naprawde-mowi-ai-act-i-co-zmienia-sie-2-sierpnia-2026.html).
- **EU Digital Fairness Act:** no proposal had been tabled as of Sept 2026; one is expected in Q4 2026, likely covering influencer-marketing transparency — [Streamlex DFA tracker](https://streamlex.eu/resources/eu-digital-fairness-act-tracker-updates-and-timeline/); [Freshfields](https://technologyquotient.freshfields.com/post/102juif/digital-fairness-fitness-check-and-digital-fairness-actpart-4-social-media-comm); [BEUC position, Dec 2025](https://www.beuc.eu/sites/default/files/publications/BEUC-X-2025-110_Towards_the_Digital_Fairness_Act.pdf). **Proposed, not adopted.**

### Inferences
- **Forbidden or very high risk:** an AI actor presented as a real buyer ("kupiłam to i jestem zachwycona", "moja opinia po miesiącu"). Also: on-screen star ratings or "opinie klientów" fabricated with AI, and gift-reaction skits captioned as "prawdziwa reakcja" ("real reaction"). These fit UPNPR misleading-action rules and likely black-list 23c ("nieprawdziwe rekomendacje"). An AI label does not cure them.
- **Safer framing for AI "UGC" formats:**
  1. Make it an openly branded presentation. A narrator or "brand character" speaks for Vellano ("W Vellano robimy zawieszki…", "Zobacz, jak błyszczy herb"), not as a customer.
  2. Label it as fiction or a skit plus AI, e.g. "Scenka reklamowa. Postacie i głos wygenerowane przez AI".
  3. Keep commercial disclosure ("Reklama Vellano") per UOKiK's two-level approach: in-video and the platform toggle.
  4. Make only verifiable product claims: material, size, licence, packaging, delivery dates.
  5. If using real reviews, quote genuine verified customers, with documentation and consent, and never re-voice them with AI actors as if they were the speaker.
- Gift-reaction skits are acceptable as obvious dramatised scenes ("tak może wyglądać reakcja na prezent"). They are not acceptable as "authentic customer moments".
- Product depiction must match reality (rhinestone sparkle, size, chain type). An AI-beautified pendant is both an AI Act deepfake issue and a misleading-advertising issue.

### Gaps
- No UOKiK decision, statement or guidance specifically on AI avatars, synthetic UGC or deepfake ads was found. UOKiK's own site could not be accessed to confirm.
- The exact numbering and wording of the black-list points in the current Polish UPNPR annex (Dz.U. 2023 poz. 845) was not verified.

## 5. Using a real person's likeness (friends) in AI-animated ads: GDPR and Polish image rights (art. 81 pr. aut., dobra osobiste) — what consent is needed?

### Takeaway
Publishing a recognisable person's image in an ad requires their **permission under art. 81 ustawy o prawie autorskim**. It must not be presumed, and the publisher bears the burden of proving it. This sits alongside **personal-rights protection (art. 23–24 k.c.)** and a separate **GDPR legal basis** for processing the image (biometric-style use in AI tools). A general consent to "photos" or to the original pictures does not automatically cover **AI animation, synthetic avatars, voice cloning or putting new words in their mouth**. The safest approach is a written, specific licence or consent covering AI modification, advertising use, channels (TikTok/Meta paid ads), territory, duration, and withdrawal. Under TikTok rules, the likeness of private adults without permission is prohibited, and under the AI Act such content is a deepfake that must be labelled.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 81 requires the depicted person's permission. The existence and scope of consent cannot be presumed, and the burden of proof lies on the person disseminating the image. Posing for a photo is not consent to publication. Narrow exceptions (art. 81 ust. 2): paid posing without reservation, public figures in their public role, or a person being only a detail of a larger whole (crowd, landscape) — [LexDigital](https://lexdigital.pl/ochrona-wizerunku/); [Plusuj.pl](https://plusuj.pl/rozpowszechnianie-wizerunku-bez-zgody-art-81-kara-co-grozi/); [Wezwaniedozaprzestania.pl](https://wezwaniedozaprzestania.pl/wizerunek-bez-zgody-wezwanie-do-usuniecia-zdjecia/).
- Deepfakes can infringe both art. 81 and personal rights (art. 23–24 k.c.), often by putting a person in a false context. Liability falls on those who disseminate — [Kancelaria Graś i Wspólnicy](https://kglegal.pl/roszczenia-odszkodowawcze-z-tytulu-bezprawnego-wykorzystania-wizerunku-w-technologii-deepfake/); [GazetaPrawna](https://edgp.gazetaprawna.pl/prawo/prawo-internetu-i-ochrony-danych/artykuly/11225472,czy-prawo-pozwala-chronic-wizerunek-w-erze-deepfakeow.html); [Amelica (academic analysis)](https://portal.amelica.org/ameli/journal/463/4633633001/html/).
- Art. 81 permission is **not** GDPR consent. Both regimes run in parallel: copyright law governs dissemination, and GDPR governs processing of the image as personal data — [Blog Dane Osobowe](https://blog-daneosobowe.pl/przetwarzanie-wizerunku-rodo/); [PismaRODO](https://pismarodo.pl/baza-wiedzy/zgoda-na-wykorzystanie-wizerunku/).
- GDPR consent must be freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous. It can be withdrawn at any time, as easily as it was given (art. 7(3) GDPR), with effect for the future. The business must be able to stop further use quickly — [PismaRODO](https://pismarodo.pl/baza-wiedzy/zgoda-na-wykorzystanie-wizerunku/); [BPPZ](https://bppz.pl/zgoda-na-wykorzystanie-wizerunku-czy-jej-potrzebujesz/).
- GDPR does not require written form, but consent must be unambiguous and provable. Written documentation is recommended for evidence — [PismaRODO](https://pismarodo.pl/baza-wiedzy/zgoda-na-wykorzystanie-wizerunku/); [Blog Dane Osobowe](https://blog-daneosobowe.pl/przetwarzanie-wizerunku-rodo/).
- Consent to a traditional recording does not automatically cover creating a synthetic avatar, voice cloning, or generating new statements. The agreement should specify purpose, duration, channels, territories, translation/modification rights, retention of samples, and the withdrawal procedure. If an avatar vendor stores the likeness, the vendor also needs a basis — [Ovidia](https://ovidia.pl/reklama-wideo-ai-prawo/); [Grupa Tense](https://www.grupatense.pl/blog/bezpieczenstwo-danych-w-marketingu-ai/) (secondary, practitioner blogs).
- TikTok bars AI content using the likeness of private adults without permission, even when labelled — [TikTok AI Safety Essentials PDF](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/AI_Safety_Essentials_for_Advertisers_on_TikTok.pdf).

### Inferences
- The friends' existing oral or informal consent "to use photos" is probably **insufficient** for paid AI-animated ads. Get a **signed written consent/licence** per person covering:
  - (a) use of image and, if applicable, voice in paid advertising on TikTok, Meta, the website and the shop;
  - (b) AI processing: animation, generated movement or expressions, synthetic speech;
  - (c) no use in contexts that could harm their reputation;
  - (d) the term (e.g., 12–24 months) and territory;
  - (e) remuneration, or an explicit statement that use is free of charge;
  - (f) the right to withdraw, and how quickly ads will be taken down;
  - (g) consent to upload their photos to named AI vendors (GDPR processor terms or the vendor's ToS).

  Keep the documents. TikTok or Meta may ask for proof if the person reports the ad.
- Do not make friends "say" that they bought or recommend the product unless true and agreed (Q4). Label the animated clips as AI (Q1).
- Withdrawal risk: build ads so that a friend's clip can be swapped out quickly.

### Gaps
- No Polish court ruling specific to AI-animated likeness in advertising was found. The exact form needed (written vs documentary) for art. 81 permission was not settled by the sources. A lawyer should draft the consent form.

## 6. Trademarks and licensing: typical football-club licence restrictions on advertising; do TikTok/Meta require proof of rights for ads showing club crests; avoiding IP takedowns; public FC Barcelona brand guidelines

### Takeaway
No public FC Barcelona, Real Madrid or Manchester United licensee advertising manual was found. Standard merchandise-licence practice is consistent:
- the licensor approves **all advertising and promotional materials** before release, typically with a fixed review window;
- strict quality control applies to logo use (no altered colours or proportions, official artwork only);
- endorsement or "official partner" status may not be implied;
- player names and images are excluded unless separately licensed (they belong to players or their image-rights companies);
- co-branding needs prior written approval;
- other bodies' marks (league, competition) are not covered.

TikTok's ad policy forbids third-party brands "without authorization", and ads may be stopped after rights-holder complaints. No ads-side upload of licence documents was documented, but TikTok Shop uses Brand Authorization. Vellano should keep its licence and approvals ready and get licensor sign-off on AI ads, especially where AI renders the crest.

### Cited Findings
- **FC Barcelona:** the club is member-owned and changes to its identity need members' approval. A 2018 crest redesign was dropped after membership opposition — [Barça Innovation Hub](https://barcainnovationhub.fcbarcelona.com/blog/fc-barcelona-brand-strategy/); [Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/24-30-september-2018/250541-2/); [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2018/10/03/barcelona-football-club-crest-rebrand-design/). A design agency states it authored a trilingual **FC Barcelona Digital Brand Guide** for web, apps and e-commerce for third-party licensees. This confirms a licensee guide exists, but it is not public — [Jivaldi case study](https://www.jivaldi.com/casestudy/fcb/). A design blog says commercial use requires a formal agreement with the club's licensing department and that promotional materials need approval, with rules such as official files, clear space, and no colour or proportion changes — [Design Your Way](https://www.designyourway.net/blog/barcelona-logo/) (secondary, low authority).
- **Manchester United** actively enforces its name and crest. It sued over unlicensed use in Football Manager, arguing that using the name even without the logo "deprives the registered proprietor of its right to have the club crest licensed" — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/manchester-united-sues-football-manager-over-trademark-infringement-and-mod-support/); [Pinepat](https://www.pinepat.com/en/insights/columns/sports-licensing-ip-protection-why-man-utd-is-man-red-in-fm). United's licensing structure includes an Adidas deal with dual-branding licensing rights — [Licensing International](https://licensinginternational.org/news/manchester-uniteds-merchandising-and-licensing-revenue-triples/). Real Madrid uses regional licensing agents — [License Global](https://www.licenseglobal.com/sports/eye-prize-look-sports-licensing-europe).
- **Typical licence clauses:**
  - Licensors approve "all licensed merchandise and all related packaging and advertising materials before any merchandise or advertisement is released to the public" — [Outside GC](https://outsidegc.com/blog/practical-considerations-in-merchandise-licensing-agreements/).
  - Sample clauses require submitting advertising concepts for approval, e.g. within a 10-business-day review window — [SEC exhibit (licence agreement)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1424100/000135007113000058/ex105aplicagr.htm); [Koehser](http://www.dklex.com/the-basics-of-a-merchandise-license-agreement.html).
  - Trademark licences need quality-control provisions, covering logo placement, colours and labelling — [Cohn Legal](https://www.cohnlg.com/how-does-trademark-licensing-work-for-sports-teams-and-athletes-merchandise-and-brand-licensing-explained/).
  - The licence "does not constitute and may not be used so as to imply the endorsement" of the licensee's products by the licensor, and co-branding requires prior written approval — [SEC exhibit (trademark licence)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/26820/000119312508253523/dex101.htm).
  - Marks of governing bodies are not included and need separate approval — [Outside GC](https://outsidegc.com/blog/practical-considerations-in-merchandise-licensing-agreements/) (from search summary of sample clauses).
- **Player image rights** are normally licensed separately, often via the player's image-rights company. Using a player's image to imply endorsement is prohibited unless agreed — [World Football Index](https://worldfootballindex.com/2025/08/what-are-footballers-image-rights-and-how-do-they-work/); [The Football Week, Sept 2026](https://thefootballweek.com/2026/09/12/football-image-rights/); [TrueRights](https://truerights.com/knowledge-hub/from-pitch-to-poster-usage-rights-and-footballers-likeness-in-the-premier-league).
- **TikTok ads IP policy:** "We do not allow the use of third-party brands without authorization". It bars "display or use of unauthorized third-party names, logos, or brands in a way that could mislead users about your brand affiliation", as well as counterfeits and replicas — [TikTok Ads Policy: Intellectual Property Infringement](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-intellectual-property-infringement); [IP rules for ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/intellectual-property-infringement-rules-ads). TikTok **Shop** requires Brand Authorization via the Qualification Center for certain branded products and may request licence or invoice evidence — [TikTok Shop: Requirements for Brand Authorization](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=2419888845686570&lang=en); [TikTok Shop IP Policy](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6837901778306818&lang=en). No ads-side list of required documents was found.
- **Meta:** ads may not infringe third-party trademarks. Enforcement is often complaint-driven, so an ad can run and then be stopped after a rights-holder report — [Bir.ch guide](https://bir.ch/blog/what-can-you-advertise-on-meta); [Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/17-topics-that-result-in-rejected-ads-on-facebook-and-instagram/) (secondary).

### Inferences
- **Before producing AI ads**, re-read each licence (FCB, Real Madrid, Man Utd, or their agents) for clauses on:
  - (a) approval of advertising and digital or social content;
  - (b) whether AI-generated depictions of the crest are allowed, since AI often distorts crests (wrong stripes, letters, colours). That breaches quality-control clauses and invites takedowns;
  - (c) the permitted wording, e.g. "oficjalnie licencjonowany produkt FC Barcelona" vs forbidden "oficjalny partner/sponsor";
  - (d) required licence notices, hologram or licence-number display;
  - (e) territory (Poland/EU) and channels (paid social).
- **Low-risk practice:** composite the **real product photo or real crest artwork** onto AI scenes instead of letting the generator draw the crest. Keep crests exactly as licensed. Avoid players, player names or shirt numbers, stadium imagery, club anthems or slogans ("Més que un club", "Hala Madrid", "Glory Glory"), kit-sponsor logos, and league or competition marks (LaLiga, Premier League, UEFA Champions League). Each is likely a separately owned mark or right. Don't imply the club endorses Vellano. Don't put the crest on AI people in ways that could look like official club campaigns.
- Keep a PDF pack (licence certificate or letter, licensor approvals, invoices from the licensed manufacturer if Vellano is a reseller) ready for TikTok or Meta appeals and rights-holder disputes.
- If Vellano is a **reseller** of a licensee's products rather than the licensee itself, its right to use crests in ads derives from exhaustion and reseller advertising rules. These are narrower: product-focused advertising only, no implied affiliation. Confirm with the licensor or a lawyer which situation applies.

### Gaps
- No public FCB, Real Madrid or Man Utd licensee marketing guidelines were found. Real approval terms must come from Vellano's own licence agreements or the licensor.
- No confirmation whether any club registered "Més que un club" or similar slogans as trademarks (my background assumption, not verified).
- TikTok and Meta ad-side proof-of-licence procedures are not documented publicly in the sources found.

## 7. Music in ads: trending sounds vs commercial music libraries on TikTok and Meta business accounts

### Takeaway
Business and brand content, including all paid ads, must use **commercially cleared music**. On TikTok this means the **Commercial Music Library (CML)**; business accounts see CML only, and trending or chart sounds from the general library are not licensed for promotion. On Meta it means the **Meta Sound Collection** or independently licensed tracks. Platform licences are platform-specific: a CML track is not cleared for Meta, and vice versa. AI-generated music needs checking against the generator's commercial-use terms.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok's general Sounds may not be used "to promote, advertise, sponsor, or associate music with a brand or business" unless the user has the rights. Business accounts can't put trending or general-library tracks into promotional content, and the in-app editor shows CML only — [Soundstripe: Why can I only use commercial sounds](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/why-can-i-only-use-commercial-sounds-on-tiktok); [Soundstripe: TikTok music licensing rules](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/tiktok-music-licensing-rules); [Audiodrome](https://audiodrome.net/for-creators/tiktok-commercial-sounds/).
- CML: over 1 million tracks, mostly independent artists rather than major-label hits. Cleared for organic brand posts and paid TikTok ads at no per-track cost, but **TikTok placements only**. Check territory and ad-format availability — [Foxi Music](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/commercial-music-licensing-tiktok-guide/); [Third Chair](https://usethirdchair.com/blog/tiktok-commercial-library-what-brands-can-really-use); [Velveteen](https://www.velveteen.fm/guides/tiktok-for-musicians/tiktok-commercial-music-library-explained). Violations can lead to muting, removal or account penalties — [Foxi Music](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/commercial-music-licensing-tiktok-guide/).
- Meta: business accounts are limited to the **Meta Sound Collection** or their own commercially licensed music. The consumer music library is licensed for non-commercial use only and can't be used in ads. Boosted Reels with consumer-library music fail. Sound Collection tracks are royalty-free for commercial use, including ads, but licensed for Meta products only — [Foxi Music (Instagram)](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/instagram-reels-music-copyright-legal-guide/); [Audiodrome (Facebook)](https://audiodrome.net/for-creators/royalty-free-music-for-facebook/); [Shuttergen](https://shuttergen.com/resources/meta-music-library); [Tally Terms](https://tallyterms.com/blog/music-in-sponsored-and-business-posts). Meta's music guidelines prohibit using music for commercial or non-personal purposes without appropriate licences — quoted by [Sirency](https://www.sirency.com/blog/instagram-music-business-account) (secondary; Meta terms reportedly last modified March 2022).
- Sources conflict on the Sound Collection's size (about 14,000 vs about 16,000 tracks) and on whether some business accounts lose access — [Audiodrome](https://audiodrome.net/for-creators/royalty-free-music-for-facebook/); [Sirency](https://www.sirency.com/blog/instagram-music-business-account).

### Inferences
- Ad production rule: render the master video **without** music, then add the CML track inside TikTok Ads Manager or the editor for TikTok, and a Sound Collection or separately licensed track for Meta. Alternatively, buy one cross-platform sync licence from a stock library such as Epidemic or Artlist that explicitly covers paid social.
- Don't imitate trending songs with AI. A sound-alike of a famous track, or of club anthems or chants, can still infringe composition rights.
- AI voiceover and AI music generator outputs: check the tool's licence permits commercial advertising use.

### Gaps
- TikTok's and Meta's official music terms pages could not be fetched. All details come from secondary licensing-vendor blogs.

## 8. Polish price and discount rules (Omnibus: lowest price in the previous 30 days) for ads mentioning "2 za 259 zł" or Black Friday

### Takeaway
Under the Polish implementation of PID art. 6a (Omnibus), any communication that **shows or even suggests a price reduction** of a product must also state the **lowest price in the 30 days before the reduction**, labelled exactly in substance as "najniższa cena z 30 dni przed obniżką". This applies **in ads too**, and the % or zł saving must be calculated from that 30-day low (CJEU C-330/23 Aldi Süd, 26 Sept 2024). A neutral multi-buy price ("2 zawieszki za 259 zł") with no reduction claim is arguably a bundle price, not a reduction. Framing it as a saving ("oszczędzasz 39 zł", "taniej", strike-through, "-15%", "Black Friday −20%") triggers the rule. UOKiK has warned against using bundles or loyalty mechanisms to circumvent it.

### Cited Findings
- UOKiK President's explanations (Dec 2022, supplemented 8 May 2023): the obligation applies to every trader that promotes goods by **showing or even suggesting a reduction**, including on platforms and price comparators, with discount codes, and in loyalty programmes. Merely lowering a price without drawing attention to it creates no extra obligation — [UOKiK: Informacja o obniżce ceny – Wyjaśnienia Prezesa UOKiK 2023 (PDF)](https://uokik.gov.pl/storage/archiwum/e6e552a1.pdf); [KPMG Legal Alert](https://kpmg.com/pl/pl/home/insights/2023/05/legal-alert-omnibus-ponownie-wyjasnienia-prezesa-uokik-odnosnie-informacji-o-obnizce-ceny.html).
- The lowest price must be clearly shown and described as "najniższa cena z 30 dni przed obniżką". Labels such as "cena referencyjna" or "cena omnibus" are not acceptable. It must appear wherever a reduction on a specific product is announced, **including advertisements**, leaflets, shelf labels, conditional sales and loyalty programmes — [Radio Kraków](https://www.radiokrakow.pl/aktualnosci/uokik-najnizsza-cena-z-30-dni-przed-obnizka-musi-byc-czytelna-i-jasno-opisana); [GazetaPrawna](https://www.gazetaprawna.pl/firma/artykuly/11190788,uokik-czytelna-i-jasno-opisana-najnizsza-cena-z-30-dni-przed-obnizka.html); [WIIH Bydgoszcz](https://bydgoszcz.wiih.gov.pl/index.php/tresc-artykulu-komunikaty/8-jak-obliczac-i-prezentowac-obnizki-cen-wyjasnienia-prezesa-uokik).
- UOKiK computes the % reduction from the 30-day low. Example: 30-day low 80 zł, price before promo 100 zł, promo 75 zł means "obniżka o 6,25%, a nie o 25%" — [Gekko Taxens](https://www.taxens.pl/prawo/dyrektywa-omnibus-po-raz-kolejny-przedmiotem-wyjasnien-czesc-2/); [ecommercelegal.pl](https://ecommercelegal.pl/blogs/news/dyrektywa-omnibus-co-wynika-z-najnowszych-wyjasnien-prezesa-uokik).
- For products offered for less than 30 days, the reference is the lowest price from first offer until the reduction — [Radio Kraków](https://www.radiokrakow.pl/aktualnosci/uokik-najnizsza-cena-z-30-dni-przed-obnizka-musi-byc-czytelna-i-jasno-opisana).
- UOKiK observed **loyalty programmes or bundled offers** being used to avoid the 30-day rule — [GazetaPrawna](https://www.gazetaprawna.pl/firma/artykuly/11190788,uokik-czytelna-i-jasno-opisana-najnizsza-cena-z-30-dni-przed-obnizka.html). One article says offers like "kup dwa produkty, trzeci gratis" are outside the rule — [Money.pl](https://www.money.pl/gospodarka/jak-pokazywac-obnizki-cen-uokik-opublikowal-instrukcje-dla-sklepow-6895764265274112a.html) (attribution from search summary; not confirmed against the UOKiK text). Conflict: these two points pull in different directions, and no source addressed "2 za X zł" directly.
- **CJEU C-330/23 Aldi Süd (26 Sept 2024):** a reduction announced as a percentage, or as a promotional statement stressing an advantageous price, must be determined from the lowest price in at least the 30 days before. Merely displaying the 30-day low somewhere in the ad is not enough — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/new-ecj-judgement-price-indication-directive-implications-online-retailers-and); [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2024/09/30/more-on-pricing-and-discount-claims-cjeu-clarifies-basis-for-calculating-reducti-102jkhq); [Kinstellar](https://www.kinstellar.com/news-and-insights/detail/2990/consumer-law-update-cjeu-rules-that-a-price-reduction-announced-in-an-advertisement-must-be-calculated-on-the-basis-of-the-lowest-price-in-the-last-30-days); [Noerr](https://www.noerr.com/en/insights/impact-of-the-cjeu-ruling-on-discount-advertising-in-europe-what-it-means-for-businesses).
- The rule was designed against "raise then discount" around Black Friday — [Money.pl](https://www.money.pl/gospodarka/jak-pokazywac-obnizki-cen-uokik-opublikowal-instrukcje-dla-sklepow-6895764265274112a.html). UOKiK continues to open proceedings on price-reduction presentation — [PrawoDlaBiznesu.eu](https://prawodlabiznesu.eu/jak-prawidlowo-informowac-o-obnizkach-cen-nowe-postepowania-uokik-to-sygnal-ostrzegawczy-dla-rynku/); see also [PARP: Omnibus – informowanie o cenach](https://www.parp.gov.pl/component/content/article/82715:dyrektywa-omnibus-obowiazek-informowania-o-cenach).

### Inferences
- "2 za 259 zł" shown as a plain bundle price (no "promocja", no crossed-out price, no "oszczędzasz") is the lowest-risk version. Also show the single-unit price, and keep the bundle running at that price consistently.
- If the ad says "taniej", "promocja", "−X%", shows "zamiast 298 zł", or says "Black Friday", show "Najniższa cena z 30 dni przed obniżką: … zł" for each product concerned. Calculate the advertised saving from that 30-day low, and keep price-history evidence (Shopify price logs or screenshots).
- Black Friday: freeze prices for at least 30 days before the event, and never raise prices in October/November to "discount" them later.
- AI voiceovers announcing prices or discounts must match the on-screen and checkout prices exactly. Avoid fake countdown timers or "ostatnie sztuki" claims unless true; UOKiK's DRAKE tool targets such dark patterns (Q4).
- Statutory basis (from background knowledge, verify): art. 4 ust. 2–5 ustawy z 9 maja 2014 r. o informowaniu o cenach towarów i usług, as amended from 1 Jan 2023.

### Gaps
- No UOKiK text addressing "2 za X zł" multi-buy pricing specifically was found. A lawyer should confirm whether presenting a bundle price as a deal counts as a reduction.
- No 2025–2026 UOKiK fines specific to Omnibus price displays were confirmed in the sources found.

## 9. Synthesis: compliance checklist per planned Vellano ad format (what must be disclosed, what is forbidden, what is risky)

### Takeaway
Every realistic AI human, voice or scene needs a **visible Polish AI label from the first second**. On TikTok, also switch on the **AIGC disclaimer**. On Meta, rely on the in-video label; Meta's own label is not guaranteed. AI actors must never pose as real customers. Friends' likeness requires written AI-specific consent. Crests must be used exactly as licensed, with licensor approval, and with no implied club endorsement and no players. Music must come from CML or the Sound Collection (or be separately licensed). Any discount wording triggers the 30-day lowest-price display.

### Cited Findings
- Deployers must label realistic AI content at first exposure; invented photorealistic people count; ads don't get the creative-work regime — [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/european-commission-adopts-final-guidelines-on-ai-act-article-50-transparency-obligations-first-impr); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nbz0/transparency-obligations-for-ai-generated-content-the-code-of-practice-adequacy/); [Numonic](https://www.numonic.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-article-50-content-creators); [Prawo.pl](https://www.prawo.pl/biznes/ai-act-kto-musi-oznaczac-tresci-od-2-sierpnia,1548339.html).
- Code of Practice for non-real-time deepfake video: EU icon plus a disclaimer at the start of exposure — [Heuking](https://www.heuking.de/en/news-events/newsletter-articles/detail/the-european-commission-specifies-labelling-requirements-for-ai-generated-content-and-deepfakes.html).
- TikTok: undisclosed AIGC is rejected or restricted; duplication resets the toggle; Spark Ads use the organic label; likeness of private adults without permission is banned — [TikTok ad disclaimers](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-ad-disclaimers-in-tiktok-ads-manager); [AI Safety Essentials](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/AI_Safety_Essentials_for_Advertisers_on_TikTok.pdf).
- Meta: auto "AI info" only for Meta's own gen-AI tools; third-party AI ads are not necessarily labelled — [Meta Help Center](https://www.meta.com/en-gb/help/artificial-intelligence/355108217670024/).
- Fake consumer reviews and recommendations are banned (Omnibus 23b/23c); UOKiK requires two-level, Polish-language ad labelling — [PARP](https://www.parp.gov.pl/component/content/article/83340:dyrektywa-omnibus-nowe-obowiazki-dla-e-sprzedawcow-oraz-internetowych-platform-handlowych); [UOKiK recommendations](https://uokik.gov.pl/download/26685).

### Inferences
**Per-format checklist (all are inferences built on the findings above; have a Polish lawyer and the licensors confirm):**

| Ad concept | Must disclose | Forbidden | Risky / design tips |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI person in hoodie, "3-2-1" reveal | In-video label from frame 1, e.g. "Wideo wygenerowane przez AI" plus EU icon, kept as a small persistent corner tag; TikTok AIGC toggle; "Reklama Vellano"/commercial-content toggle | Making the person look like a real footballer or celebrity; showing player names or numbers | AI-drawn crest may be distorted, so composite the real product photo; product must look as it really is |
| Person putting the chain on (close-up, neck or hands) | Same as above | Beautifying the pendant beyond reality | Hands-only real footage avoids the deepfake label for the person, but the product must still not be misrepresented |
| Talking AI "UGC" actor in Polish | Label covering both people and voice ("Postać i głos wygenerowane przez AI"); spoken mention optional but recommended | "Kupiłem/am i polecam", "moja szczera opinia", fake star ratings or "opinie klientów", "prawdziwa recenzja" | Script as brand presenter or skit: "Pokażę Ci, jak wygląda zawieszka z bliska"; only factual claims (licence, material, size, delivery) |
| Gift-reaction skit | AI label plus "scenka" framing | Captions like "prawdziwa reakcja mojego chłopaka" | Exaggerated or obviously staged comedic tone strengthens the fiction framing, but the AI label is still needed (ads ≠ "evidently artistic") |
| Product animation (rotating pendant, sparkle) | Label if photorealistic AI-generated or changed; stylised 3D/graphic motion probably not a deepfake (still label if unsure) | Rendering stones, metal or size inaccurately | Base on real photos; keep crest artwork exact per licence |
| Animated real friends (old content) | Deepfake label; consent docs on file | Use without written consent that covers AI animation and paid ads; putting endorsements in their mouths | Re-collect consent now; have an easy takedown process |
| AI voiceover only (real footage) | Label "Głos lektora wygenerowany przez AI" (conservative); TikTok AIGC toggle (AI audio counts) | Cloning a real person's voice, e.g. a commentator, without consent | Use stock synthetic voices with commercial licences |
| Crest/club references (all ads) | "Oficjalnie licencjonowany produkt [club]" only if licence allows that wording; any licence-notice text required | "Oficjalny partner/sponsor", club slogans or anthems, stadium, players, league or competition logos, implying club endorsement | Get licensor approval for each ad or template; keep the licence pack for TikTok/Meta appeals |
| Music | n/a | Trending or chart sounds from the general library; sound-alike AI of famous songs or chants | TikTok CML / Meta Sound Collection / cross-platform sync licence |
| Price messages ("2 za 259 zł", Black Friday) | If any reduction is suggested: "Najniższa cena z 30 dni przed obniżką: X zł" per product; % calculated from that | Inflating prices before Black Friday; fake timers or scarcity | Plain bundle price without "promocja" wording is lowest risk |

**Process items:**
- Keep a per-ad compliance log with: the AI tools used, the label applied, the platform toggles set, the licensor approval, the consent forms, the music licence and the price-history evidence.
- Contracts with agencies or freelancers should require AI labelling and IP clearances.
- Re-check TikTok and Meta policy pages before each campaign. Third-party summaries conflict.

### Gaps
- Whether a platform's own AI label (TikTok's in-feed disclaimer, Meta "AI info") alone satisfies Art. 50(4) "clear and distinguishable at first exposure" was not addressed by any source. The in-video label is the conservative answer.
- Actual licence terms for Vellano's FCB, Real Madrid and Man Utd products are unknown; the licensor must confirm the permitted ad wording and approval process.
- All primary texts (EUR-Lex, Dziennik Ustaw, UOKiK, TikTok/Meta policy pages) need direct verification; network access to them was blocked during this research.
